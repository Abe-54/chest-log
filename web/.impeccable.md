## Design Context

Written without a teach interview because the owner asked not to be contacted during the run. Every line comes from what the owner said in the session or from how the data is used.

### Users
Fortnite players on PC and their squads. They play Fortnite in the evening and check this between matches on a phone, or on a work laptop the next day. The job is to see how many chests a week of play produced, find the drop and route that opens the most chests fastest (he is grinding chest opens for a pirate-themed reward token), rewatch moments, and send a link to a friend.

### Brand Personality
Loud, salty, triumphant. A crew's logbook after a good raid, not an analytics product. Numbers are trophies. Copy is short and a little cocky.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Dark ink harbour at night as the base, because play and checking happen in the evening and the island map reads best on dark.
- Treasure gold is the one hero accent (my chests). Coral marks fights, kelp green marks teammates, storm violet marks the storm. No cyan-on-dark, no purple-blue gradients, no glow.
- Recap slides break the dark base with full-bleed blocks of gold, coral, kelp and bone, the way Wrapped uses flat colour fields.
- Type: Anybody (variable width) set heavy and condensed for numbers and headings, wide for the wordmark. Schibsted Grotesk for body. Tabular numerals everywhere numbers line up.
- Anti-references: generic SaaS dashboards, card grids with icons over every heading, Fortnite's own branding and fonts.

### Design Principles
1. Phone first. Every screen is designed at 390 px wide first, with thumb-reach controls at the bottom.
2. Numbers are the hero. One big number per view, supporting detail small.
3. Scrubbing must feel instant. Time drives the map directly with no waiting.
4. Every view is a link. Whatever is on screen can be shared and reopens exactly.
5. Motion only where it explains: map fly-to, slide transitions, press feedback. Nothing on repeated keyboard actions.
